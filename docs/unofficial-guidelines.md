# Unofficial Admission Guidelines

Internal committee rules and conventions that aren't documented in EPFL's formal admission policy but consistently shape MTE deliberation. Captured from committee meetings (most recently 2026-05-08).

These exist alongside — and sometimes in tension with — the official rules. Use them as agent context, not as user-facing justification.

---

## Refusal letters

Every refusal can come back to the section through EPFL — candidates increasingly request formal reasons.

- The official refusal reason must be defensible and "politically correct": academic background fit, GPA below threshold, stronger alternative programs.
- Internal-only reasons (age, cohort integration concerns, suspected weak motivation, university reputation suspicion) **must never appear** in the candidate-facing letter.
- The expert reviewer does not see internal committee remarks — keep these in committee-only notes.

## PhD applicants

EPFL **cannot admit candidates who already hold a PhD** into a Master's program. (A second Master's, by contrast, is permitted; a second PhD in a different section is also permitted.)

- Profile-fit and age / cohort-integration may be cited internally but **cannot** be used as the official refusal reason.
- The user-facing refusal must point to background fit or recommend a more appropriate program (e.g. EMBA, executive education).

## GPA bars by university

The official EPFL bar is applied per institution. Known internal exceptions / inconsistencies:

- **La Sapienza (Roma):** official bar 29/30, but Bologna and Politecnico di Milano are 28/30. The committee considers this inconsistent; an action item exists to request lowering Sapienza to 28 for next cycle. Until then, the 29 bar holds — borderline cases should be flagged, not auto-accepted.
- **Italian system note:** grades can be retaken multiple times, which inflates averages — this is the rationale for the high Italian bar.
- **Brazilian / Latin American transcripts:** "top X%" claims often refer to the entrance exam, not the cohort GPA. Verify before treating as a class-rank signal.

## Internal EPFL bachelor candidates

The 4.5 algebra-and-analysis threshold is applied more flexibly for MTE than for other programs, because grades typically rise in years 2–3 with specialised coursework.

- Standard threshold: **4.5** average across algebra + analysis.
- Minimum acceptable if the candidate is switching out of their original bachelor section: **4.0**.
- The agent must detect "mise à niveau" / section-switch trajectories — a low raw average can mask a candidate who failed advanced classes, switched section, and recovered. Read the transcript chronologically; do not auto-reject on the raw average alone.
- Advanced-class grades (e.g. Analyse avancée 1) sit on a different scale than standard equivalents. A 3.4 in advanced ≈ a 4.9 in standard. Account for this when computing comparable averages.

## Disciplinary vs interdisciplinary minors

When recommending a minor for an admitted candidate, prefer **disciplinary** minors aligned with the candidate's bachelor. Interdisciplinary minors are valid choices but should not be the default suggestion.

Never recommend the **MTE minor** (Management, technologie et entrepreneuriat) to MTE-program candidates — it overlaps with the program itself.

Up to **three minor suggestions** can be given per accepted candidate, ranked by fit. Ordering matters: the strongest disciplinary match goes first.

## Cohort and historical signals

- **Turkish candidates:** historical issues with English fluency and complaint patterns. Five accepted last year; not a hard policy, but cohort fit is examined more closely.
- **Single-country cohorts:** the committee values having at least one strong candidate from each represented country. Borderline candidates from under-represented countries may be pushed up if their case is otherwise defensible.
- **Unknown universities with strong students:** if the candidate is clearly the top of an unknown institution (e.g. multiple top-1 rankings, national prizes), they may be accepted as a wildcard. Disciplinary minor should be the safest fit (often Systems Engineering or Computer Science).

## Letters of recommendation

- Length / specificity floor: a 5-line letter from a 5-month relationship is treated as a weak signal regardless of grades.
- Gender-pronoun inconsistencies inside a letter (e.g. "he" mid-paragraph in a letter about a "she") indicate templated or copy-pasted letters — treat as negative.
- A letter that ranks the candidate "below average" on any axis is a hard negative even if other axes are positive.
